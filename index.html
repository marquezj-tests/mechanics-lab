Your first mechanics experiment

Press Run to explore the example. Pause and drag the timeline to replay recorded motion. Run resumes playback, then continues the experiment at its latest state.

Use the canvas toolbar’s Select multiple icon to tap components on/off or drag an additive selection box. Tap empty space to clear. Toggle the icon off to move the selected group. Pan canvas lets you drag anywhere to pan; toggle it off to edit again. These controls work with touch or a mouse, without keyboard modifiers.

Expand Solver & energy in Properties to see energy change and link length error. Physical work and dissipation can change energy too; compare conservative runs at finer Solver precision to assess numerical drift.

To build: add a pinned support and a mass by clicking the canvas. Choose Spring, then click two endpoints. On solids you can choose the center cross or an off-center attachment point, and edit both local coordinates in Properties. Choose Linear constraint and click a mass to restrict its motion to a line. Change the line angle in Properties.

For a moving readout, select a component and find On-canvas monitors in Properties. Choose a variable and press Attach monitor. The label follows the body, the midpoint of a link, or a force attachment point. Drag the label to adjust its offset; multiple variables can be attached. Labels update during motion and recorded replay and are saved with the experiment.

Choose Applied force and click a mass or solid to attach a red force arrow. Edit its components or drag its arrowhead. Off-center forces produce torque; optionally rotate the force direction with its body. Choose a spring or bar in the monitor selector to plot its reaction-force components and endpoint moments. Selecting a link also shows its reactions in Properties. For straight links, reaction on B is opposite to A; pulley routes can change its direction. Positive axial force means tension. Moments are about body centers. Ideal pins carry no reaction couple; fixed point supports clamp connected bars and carry reaction torque. Links are massless, so their stored momentum is zero; transmitted impulses and body momenta are available on the plot axes and in CSV. Bar reactions are impulse-averaged over the last physics step, including impact transients. Spring impulses are instantaneous-force estimates over that same step.

Use Fixed pulley to place a fixed wheel or Movable pulley to place a translating axle/load. Set its mass and optional linear guide in Properties. Select String, click a body or support, click any pulleys in order, then click a second body to finish. Strings carry tension only and may go slack. Pulley radius, axle motion and string wrap direction are editable in Properties. Alt-click a wheel to attach a string to its axle instead of routing it, or use the axle button in Properties. The Examples menu includes classical and movable-pulley Atwood machines, with acceleration and tension readouts and travel stops. Choose the pulley oscillator in Examples to try it. String tension and pulley support forces are available in plots and readouts. Choose Rigid bar, then click two bodies or anchors to connect them. Bars keep a fixed length and swivel at their pins, except where a fixed point support clamps their direction. On a rigid body, click its center cross or an off-center point to transmit torque. Bar properties allow changing length and attachment coordinates. Bars are ideal massless constraints and do not have their own collision geometry.

Add rectangles, circles, and triangles with one click. For a free polygon, click vertices in order then Finish polygon (Enter on a keyboard). Selected solids have square resize controls and outer round rotation controls at their corners. Springs attach at the center cross or at another point you choose on a solid. Edit the local attachment coordinates in Properties; off-center elastic and damping forces produce torque. Rotation snaps to 5° when snapping is enabled. Solid bodies collide, including concave polygons. Set restitution and friction in Properties, or enable Fixed body to create a floor or wall. Point masses and anchor markers do not collide. Elastic, frictionless impacts preserve rebound energy at all speeds and refine impact timing instead of pushing overlapping bodies apart. Finite timestep errors remain. Adaptive collision substeps help with fast objects; extremely fast or thin objects can still pass through each other.

Enable Snap to fine grid in Builder to place and drag bodies on the minor grid (one-fifth of the major spacing). At the initial state, green arrows show initial velocities (1 m/s spans 0.75 m). Drag the arrowheads to change direction and speed. With Snap enabled, speed changes in 0.1 m/s increments and direction in 5° increments. Constrained masses keep their line direction and snap speed only. Set zero or very small velocities in Properties. Linear constraints project velocities onto the permitted line. Reset or rewind to time zero to show the arrows. Drag objects to change initial positions. On desktop, drag empty canvas to box-select; Shift-click toggles a component and Shift-drag adds a selection box. Drag a selected body to move the group. Copy and Paste buttons or Ctrl/Cmd+C and Ctrl/Cmd+V copy components with their links, forces, and readouts. Ctrl/Cmd+A selects all; Delete removes the selection and Escape clears it. Alt-drag or middle-drag pans; touch dragging pans. Use + / − to zoom. On a phone, use the bottom tabs to open Builder and Properties.

Editing restores initial conditions and clears recordings. Save As… lets you name and download a JSON experiment file; Open restores it. Press Add plot to create a fixed, movable plot window. Each plot has an X variable and one or more Y lines; time is available for either. Use + Line to compare variables, and use each line’s checkbox, color control, or remove button to manage it. For energy comparisons, choose Mechanical energy and Kinetic energy (system). Drag its header, resize its lower-right corner, minimize or close it, and export its own CSV. Plots remain fixed on the screen while components move, and their settings are saved with the experiment.

Select a spring to set its damping coefficient (N·s/m); zero gives an undamped spring. Physics uses massless springs, point masses, and velocity Verlet integration at 240 Hz. Linear constraints are exact straight-line projections. The simulation limit is adjustable from 1 to 600 seconds (120 by default). High and Very high solver precision reduce internal steps without changing the 60 Hz recording rate. Very stiff systems can still need smaller steps; reduce stiffness or increase mass if motion becomes unstable.
